Hair Salon Industry Overview

Market size, business models, and growth outlook for the U.S. hair salon industry.

  • Industry size

    The U.S. hair salon industry generates roughly $55–60B in annual revenue across 850,000+ establishments, making it one of the largest and most fragmented segments in beauty and personal care.

  • Market trends

    Online booking, memberships, and retail add-ons are lifting average tickets, while stylist wage pressure and booth-rental models continue to reshape salon economics.

  • Typical business model

    Most salons run a commission or booth-rental model. Revenue comes from services (cut, color, styling) plus retail product sales, with a small but growing membership base.

  • Independent vs franchise

    Independents dominate by count and offer premium service pricing; franchise salons (e.g., value cut and blow-dry concepts) win on volume, systems, and marketing scale.

  • Service mix

    Color and chemical services drive the highest tickets and margins; haircuts anchor visit frequency; retail attachment and add-ons lift per-visit revenue.

  • Growth outlook

    Steady low-single-digit growth, with the strongest gains for salons that convert new clients into rebookings, memberships, and consistent retail buyers.

Revenue Drivers

The levers that move hair salon revenue — from average ticket and chair occupancy to retail and memberships.

  • Average Ticket

    Higher color and add-on mix lifts per-visit revenue and margin.

  • Number of Stylists

    More productive chairs scale service capacity and total revenue.

  • Appointments per Day

    Booking density directly drives daily and monthly revenue.

  • Retail Product Sales

    Take-home product adds 8–12% of revenue at strong margins.

  • Chair Occupancy

    Utilization of 65–80% keeps fixed costs covered per station.

  • Repeat Customers

    Retention and rebooking drive predictable, low-cost revenue.

  • Membership Revenue

    Recurring plans smooth cash flow and lock in visit frequency.

  • Upselling

    Treatments, styling, and add-ons raise ticket without new clients.

Hair salons Profitability FAQs

How profitable is a hair salon?

Typically 8 – 15% net margin (median ~11%). Top operators reach 16 – 22%.

What is the average hair salon profit margin?

About 11% net; healthy range 8 – 15%. Gross margin usually 55 – 65%.

Are hair salons profitable?

Yes — when payroll stays 45–50% of revenue and retail attachment adds 8–12% of sales. Thin margins usually signal cost drift.

How much profit does a hair salon make?

About $25K – $55K a year at median $320K revenue (~$35K at 11% net).

How much revenue does a hair salon generate?

A typical independent hair salon generates $250K–$500K in annual revenue, with a median near $320K. Larger multi-stylist hair salons and busy franchise locations can exceed $700K depending on chair count, average ticket, and utilization.

What is the average profit margin?

The average hair salon profit margin runs 8–15% net, with a median around 11%. Well-run hair salons that keep payroll near 45–50% of revenue and add strong retail attachment can push net margins to 16–22%.

How much does it cost to open a salon?

Opening a hair salon typically costs $80K–$250K, with a median near $120K. Buildout, stations, equipment, initial inventory, licensing, and working capital are the biggest line items for a new hair salon.

How much does a salon owner make?

A hair salon owner-operator typically earns $40K–$85K, with established multi-stylist salons paying owners $90K–$140K and small chains $150K+. Owner pay depends on whether the owner also works a chair and on the salon's commission vs booth-rent model.

What is a good EBITDA margin?

A healthy hair salon EBITDA margin is roughly 10–18%. Salons below that range usually carry high rent or payroll, while top operators exceed 18% through disciplined labor cost control and retail sales.

How much is a salon worth?

Most hair salons sell for 1.8–3.0× SDE (seller's discretionary earnings), or about 0.4–0.7× annual revenue. A typical hair salon is worth $180K–$420K, with value driven by profitability, client retention, and transferable staff.

How many stylists does an average salon employ?

An average hair salon employs 4–8 stylists plus front-desk and assistant staff. Revenue per stylist typically runs $60K–$120K in annual service revenue, so stylist productivity is one of the strongest drivers of salon revenue.

How long does it take to break even?

Most new hair salons break even within 12–24 months once chair utilization reaches 65–80% and rebooking builds a repeat client base. Salons that ramp bookings and retention quickly reach break-even sooner.
